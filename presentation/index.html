AI Workout Coach
Step 01 · Framing

Most apps tell you what you did. Ours tells you when to stop.

An AI coach that watches you train with your phone camera and smartwatch. It recognizes the exercise, checks your form, and compares today's session with your own past sessions. When fatigue builds up, it tells you to stop before you get hurt.

Step 07 · Model 3 · our contribution

Model 3: are you about to get hurt?

Already visible in MM-Fit: reps get slower inside a set
Input

Rep speed and depth (Model 1), form score (Model 2), heart rate (watch), and your past sessions.

The model

An LSTM or Transformer over the sequence of reps, comparing today with your own history.

How it learns without injury labels

Later reps in a set are more tired than early ones; our effort scores (1–10) confirm it.

Main dataset: MM-Fit

Public, free (mmfit.github.io). People train while a camera, two smartwatches, a phone and an earbud record them at the same time. Every number below is read from the files in our project folder.

What the labels give us

  • One row = one set: start frame, end frame, rep count, exercise.
  • Frames are video frames at 30 fps. Every sensor file has the same frame column, so this is how all the modalities are synced.
  • The rep count is given, but not where each rep starts.

What is missing

  • No form label. Nobody marked a rep as good or bad.
  • No injury or effort label.
  • Heart rate is missing in sessions ().

These gaps are why we add two more sources (next step).

Step 06 · Pipeline

Pose extraction: video → skeleton

Each frame becomes 18 joints with (x, y) pixel coordinates. We do not train this part. Pose estimation already exists, so we reuse MediaPipe or YOLO-Pose and spend our training on what is new.

Click a joint in the table to locate it on the skeleton.

One real frame from w00_pose_2d.npy

MM-Fit uses the 18-joint OpenPose layout. MediaPipe outputs 33 landmarks, so we map them onto the same 18 joints so that live data matches training data.

Backup · Pipeline

Where to put the phone

The pose estimator finds the joints from any angle, but what it can see changes with the angle. We tested it on EC3D: a simulated phone at every angle around the person, and a quick classifier that only sees that 2D view.

Phone position, seen from above
  • About 45° in front of you, so both knees and your depth are visible at once.
  • Phone at hip height, 2–3 m away, whole body in the frame.
  • Same spot every session, so a change in your numbers means fatigue, not a moved camera.
Mistake recognized on an unseen person, by camera angle
SquatLunge

Real cameras in EC3D (OpenPose, 4 cameras around the person)

The camera facing the person sees all 25 joints in about half the squat and lunge frames. The three angled cameras never see all 25, and the far arm is hidden in up to 75 % of frames. In real life a pure side view also hides the far leg, which our simulation ignores, so side views are even worse than the chart shows.

Quick test with a random forest on 2D joint positions, 4 people, train on 3 and test on the 4th. Front and back give the same 2D outline, which is why 0° and 180° match.
