# Model 2 · squat good/bad with real gym data (Mendeley)

Date 2026-11-21. Data: 2116 Mendeley squat reps from 25 people (gym, front/side/diagonal, labels per video) + 1848 EC3D squat rep-views (lab, 4 people, 7 yaws × mirror).
Test: 5-fold cross-validation grouped by person: every number is on Mendeley people the model never saw. Threshold 0.5. Generated by `python -m awc.training.train_m2_squat`.

## Reading (hand-written, 2026-11-21; regenerating the file drops this section)

- **The lab data alone does not transfer to the gym.** Trained on EC3D only, both models call every gym squat bad (balanced acc 0.50). This is the same failure as on our own videos (P(correct) 0.06).
- **Real gym data fixes most of it.** With Mendeley reps, the random forest reaches 0.725 balanced accuracy on unseen people, and 0.76 on whole sets (mean of the reps).
- **The random forest beats the ST-GCN** (0.725 vs 0.671 Mendeley-only; 0.696 vs 0.626 with EC3D). 2,000 reps is still small for a graph network on CPU (15 epochs). This matches step 2.2, where the forest also matched the ST-GCN.
- **Adding EC3D slightly hurts on gym people** (0.725 → 0.696 for the RF). The lab reps (fixed camera, 4 people, 31% correct) pull the decision toward "bad". They are still useful because they carry the mistake type.
- **Own correct squats:** called correct 9% → 59–68% (RF) and 97% (ST-GCN). The ST-GCN's 97% is not proof that it is better: these 34 reps are all correct, so a model leaning toward "good" also scores high here, and on Mendeley the ST-GCN is weaker.
- **Shortcut check, broken down:** standing body proportions alone give 0.565 and stance width alone 0.544 (same folds, RF). Good and bad videos differ a little in camera or stance, so a few points of the 0.725 may be setup. Most of it is the movement.
- **Saved model:** `models/m2_squat_rf.pickle` = EC3D + Mendeley RF (best on our own squats among the forests, close to the best on Mendeley). `models/m2_squat_stgcn.pt` = the ST-GCN, for comparison.

## Limits

- Mendeley labels are per video: every rep of a bad video counts as bad, so some label noise is expected.
- Binary only (correct / not). The mistake type still comes from EC3D.
- Our own squats are few (all labelled correct), so they test false alarms only, not detection of bad reps.
- Reps are cut by our rep counter; on Mendeley squats the 3 camera angles agree within 2 reps on 70% of sets.
