The project in one line

An AI coach that watches you train with a phone camera and a smartwatch, recognises the exercise, counts reps, checks the form, and (week 3) says stop before fatigue turns into injury.

iPhone video + watch HR→MediaPipe skeleton (18 joints)→Model 1 exercise + reps→Model 2 form→Model 3 fatigue (W3)→coach

Week 2 in five words

Real recordings, honest transfer test. Week 1 trained on public datasets; week 2 asks whether the models still work on our phone, in our gym, on our bodies (research question RQ4).

1Week 2 · technical approach (the story)

1 · The goal

In week 1 the models were trained and tested on datasets (MM-Fit for Model 1, EC3D for Model 2). Week 2 had to deliver: Model 2 (form), streaming (Kafka), the Spark rep table, and above all a test on our own real recordings. The research question RQ4 is: how much accuracy do we lose when we move from a dataset to a real phone camera?

2 · We built the capture system (the "gym station")

The iPhone opens a page in Safari (no app to install). Because Safari only gives camera access over HTTPS, the laptop runs an HTTPS server (FastAPI) with a certificate made by mkcert; the iPhone trusts the mkcert root once, and the laptop re-issues its own certificate at every start for its current IP (home Wi-Fi, gym Wi-Fi or iPhone hotspot). The phone page is protected by a PIN. While recording, the phone's MediaRecorder sends the video every second in small pieces; the laptop writes them in order and acknowledges each one, so a Wi-Fi drop loses nothing. The laptop shows a smooth live view through WebRTC. The Galaxy Watch 7 sends heart rate over Bluetooth (app "Heart for Bluetooth", standard heart-rate service 0x180D) and the laptop reads it with bleak. Phone and laptop clocks are synchronised NTP-style, so video, heart rate and labels share one clock.

3 · We recorded and cleaned the labels

Two people, one day, 20 sets, 9 exercises. Sometimes we pressed the wrong exercise button, so the written note of each set is the truth: a manifest (data/own_manifest.csv) maps every set to its true exercise, never editing the raw files. The rep box was pre-filled with 10, so rep counts are being re-checked by hand with a review tool; rep numbers are marked provisional until then.

4 · From video to skeleton

For every set, MediaPipe extracts 33 landmarks per frame, mapped to an 18-joint skeleton (the same one used for MM-Fit and EC3D). Frames are resampled to 30 fps on the real frame times recorded by the station. The skeleton is normalised: centred on the hips and scaled by the torso length, so the camera distance and the person's size don't matter. Heart rate is joined to each frame by nearest timestamp.

5 · Testing the models on our videos (zero-shot = no training on our data)

Model 1 (transformer on 3-second windows of skeleton) votes over the windows of each set. Result: 14/20 sets (70%), perfect on push-ups, squats, shoulder press, bicep curls and jumping jacks, wrong on triceps, lateral raises, sit-ups and rows. All three architectures fail on the same sets, so the errors are systematic. The diagnosis (plotting our joint trajectories against MM-Fit's) shows different variants or camera views: cable push-down instead of overhead triceps, one-arm rows from the side, etc.

Fix tested: leave-one-person-out adaptation: fine-tune on MM-Fit + one person's sets, test on the other person. Yassine: 75% → 92%; Iheb: unchanged at 62.5%. The test person is never used in training.

6 · Model 2 (form)

On EC3D, with a fair leave-one-person-out test, the random forest on 2D joint-angle features reaches squat 54%, lunge 72%, plank 83%. On real phone videos it is not calibrated: it flags our correct squats as "front bent" (P(correct) 0.06), but it correctly calls a public clip of half squats "not low enough" on 10/10 reps. So it catches clear mistakes but raises false alarms; the live coach hides it until calibration (week 3).

7 · Big data and live

Spark builds one row per rep (speed, range of motion, duration, heart rate, form score) and within-set baselines with window functions: 5,918 reps for MM-Fit, 269 for our data. Kafka streams pose and heart rate (replay: 29.8 msg/s, 48 ms latency). After each set the station runs the whole chain in the background and shows the result about 8 s later.

The message: the models work where our gym looks like their training data; where it doesn't, we found why and showed how to fix it. Everything is tested honestly: our videos are test data only.

3Answers for the professor

Q · Why only 70% on your videos?
Domain shift. The training data uses other exercise variants and a front camera. All three models fail on the same sets, and the trajectory plots show why (e.g. our triceps are a cable push-down). Adapting on one person and testing on the other raises Yassine from 75% to 92%.
Q · Is 20 sets enough?
No, it's a first signal. That's why we give a 95% confidence interval (48–85%) and count per person. Week 3 adds more sessions and people.
Q · Did you tune anything on your test videos?
No. Our videos are test data only. The adaptation experiment trains on one person and tests on the other person, never the same one. Rep-counter parameters were not changed on our videos.
Q · How do you know the true labels?
Each set has a typed exercise and a written note. When the button was wrong, the note is the truth; a manifest records every correction, and the raw files are never edited. Reps are being re-counted by hand from the videos with a review tool.
Q · How does Model 1 work?
MediaPipe gives 18 joints per frame. We normalise the skeleton (hip-centred, torso-scaled), cut 3-second windows (90 frames), and a small transformer classifies each window into 10 exercises + "no exercise". The set label is a majority vote over its windows. In week 1 it can also use the watch motion (IMU).
Q · How does Model 2 work?
EC3D 3D reps are projected to 2D from 7 camera angles (0–90°) and mirrored, to look like phone skeletons. We compute joint-angle features (knee angle, hip depth, trunk lean, knee position, stance width…) and a random forest predicts "correct" or the named mistake. Deep models (ST-GCN, LSTM) were not better, so the random forest stays.
Q · Can Model 2 detect a mistake on real video?
Partly: on a public half-squat clip it says "not low enough" on all 10 reps. But on correct squats it raises false alarms, so it isn't calibrated for phone skeletons. The fix is to record our own correct and wrong reps and calibrate with a person split (week 3). Until then the live coach hides form.
Q · How do you count reps?
From the skeleton we build a movement signal for the exercise (hip height for squats, wrist relative to shoulder for curls…), smooth it, and count its peaks with a minimum distance. Alternating exercises are counted per arm.
Q · Where is the Big Data part?
Spark joins MM-Fit video pose, watch motion and heart rate (70.8 M sensor rows) into one table, and builds the per-rep table with window functions for personal baselines. Kafka streams pose and heart rate as topics and the coach consumes them.
Q · How do video and heart rate stay in sync?
Everything is on the laptop clock. The phone's clock offset is measured with NTP-style pings (round trip 3.5 ms); each video frame's time comes from the video file plus the recorder start time converted to the laptop clock; heart rate is timestamped on arrival.
Q · What's the weakest part?
Watch heart rate (about 50% coverage, the watch goes silent) and Model 2 calibration. Both are known, measured, and planned for week 3.
Q · Why the demo videos? Are they real?
Yes: our own recordings, processed frame by frame using only past frames, as it would run live. The overlay shows the truth in grey and a ✓ or ✗. Failures are shown on purpose.

5Commands for the demo

  1. Presentation: open reports/validation2/AI_Workout_Coach_Week2.html in Chrome (keep the demo/ folder next to it). ← → change slide, F fullscreen, double-click a video for fullscreen. What to say per slide: docs/week2_talk.md.
  2. Live station: .venv\Scripts\python.exe -m awc.station → scan the QR with the iPhone → PIN → 30 fps → Connect camera → connect the watch → Start session → Space to start/end a set. The result appears about 8 s after the set.
  3. Check devices first: .venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\station_check.py
  4. Re-run the RQ4 evaluation: python scripts/process_recording.py --all then python scripts/eval_own_recordings.py
  5. Count reps by hand: python scripts/review_reps.py
  6. One demo video: python scripts/demo_video.py --own yassine/2026-11-10_SQUAT/1
If something fails live: say it calmly and switch to the recorded demo videos (slides 7–11). Firewall must allow port 8443 on all networks; if the phone can't connect, use the iPhone hotspot and restart the station after the laptop joins it.
