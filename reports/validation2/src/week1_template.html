Step 04 · Week 1 · big data

Spark puts three streams on one clock

The camera gives 30 skeletons per second, the watch 100 motion samples and about 1 heart-rate value. Spark joins them on the video frame number, one task per session. Slowed down 4×.

Camera · 30 / s Watch motion · 100 / s Heart rate · ~1 / s Synced row · one per frame
One real row of the Spark output · session w00

Watch motion is averaged onto the frame (≈3 samples each); heart rate is interpolated and masked when the nearest real value is over 5 s away. Never invented.

Step 06 · Week 1 · rep counting

Rep counting: the bug, then the fix

Lunges and curls alternate left and right. Our first counter followed the whole body and saw one left-plus-right cycle as one rep. The fix follows the right body part for each exercise. Same real set, both counters, 2× speed.

Step 05 · Week 1 · the pipeline on a real video

Real video, real model, frame by frame

Public gym videos the model has never seen, one per exercise. Every frame goes through the chain as it would live, using only the past: MediaPipe finds the skeleton, Model 1 reads the last 3 seconds and names the exercise, the rep counter counts.

  1. SkeletonMediaPipe, pretrained, 18 joints per frame
  2. Exerciseour Model 1, camera only, every 0.1 s on the last 3 s
  3. Repsour counter, with the exercise Model 1 chose

One typical video per exercise (rep error at or below the median). Over all 78 public videos of these exercises: 80.8 % recognized. RepCount-A test split (TransRAC, CVPR 2022), academic use. Same script for our own gym videos: scripts/demo_video.py.

  • One clock (the phone's) for every video frame, heart-rate value and set mark: no sync step.

  • Labels at the source: exercise, reps, effort 1–10 and form after every set.
  • Checked on a real Chrome recording: 30.2 fps, frame clock within one frame (33 ms).
